SEOUL — SEP 10

"Bloody Paradise" wins a 7th trophy and the group's first-ever triple crown, on M Countdown

On the Sept. 10 episode of Mnet's M Countdown, "Bloody Paradise" took first place for a third straight week — a first-ever triple crown for ENHYPEN — posting 7,866 points to beat BLACKPINK's Jennie and "FALLEN ANGEL." It was the song's seventh music-show win overall, following earlier trophies on Show Champion, Music Bank, Show! Music Core, Inkigayo, and M Countdown.

Summarized from Soompi by ENHYPEN Wire staff.

Read the original source ↗

SEOUL — SEP 8

Blood Saga World Tour adds a second Berlin and second London show, growing to 37 concerts in 22 cities

Citing ticket demand, the tour added an extra March 6 date at Berlin's Uber Arena and an extra March 8 date at London's The O2 — bringing Berlin onto the tour for the first time (Mar. 5–6) and giving London two nights (Mar. 8–9). With the additions, the Blood Saga World Tour — which opened in Seoul in May and has already crossed Latin America, the US, and a run through Asia — now totals 37 shows across 22 cities, wrapping in London in March 2027.

Summarized from The Korea Herald by ENHYPEN Wire staff.

Read the original source ↗

SEOUL — SEP 2

"Bloody Paradise" debuts on Billboard's Bubbling Under Hot 100, sets new career highs on the Global charts

The track entered Billboard's Bubbling Under Hot 100 at No. 23 for the week of Sept. 5 — ENHYPEN's first-ever song on that chart — while also posting career bests of No. 32 on the Global 200 (their first time cracking its top 40) and No. 12 on Global Excl. U.S. THE SIN: BLISS separately became the group's fifth album certified a "double million-seller" domestically.

Summarized from Soompi by ENHYPEN Wire staff.

SEOUL — SEP 3

"Bloody Paradise" earns a sixth music-show trophy, on M Countdown

On the Sept. 3 episode of Mnet's M Countdown, "Bloody Paradise" took its sixth win of the THE SIN: BLISS promotion cycle — ENHYPEN's 8th mini-album, released Aug. 21 — posting 7,822 points to beat BIGBANG's comeback single "BiiiG" for the trophy. The group performed as the six-member lineup of Jungwon, Jay, Jake, Sunghoon, Sunoo, and Ni-ki that has fronted the era since Heeseung's departure earlier this year.

Summarized from Soompi by ENHYPEN Wire staff.

NEW YORK — AUG 31

THE SIN: BLISS debuts at No. 1 on the Billboard 200 — a first for the group

The album became ENHYPEN's first-ever Billboard 200 chart-topper since their 2020 debut, moving roughly 98,000 units in its opening week according to Billboard's own reporting.

THE SIN: BLISS sells more than 2 million copies in its first week

Hanteo and Circle Chart tracking put first-week sales at roughly 2.09 million copies, making it one of only a handful of ENHYPEN releases — and K-pop releases generally — to cross the 2-million mark in a single opening week.

"Bloody Paradise" tops domestic and global charts for a second straight day

The title track went to No. 1 on Bugs' real-time and daily charts on release, held that position through August 23, and charted on Spotify and iTunes in over 30 countries — reaching No. 1 on iTunes in 14 of them. The music video passed 10 million YouTube views on release day.

Group confirms August comeback as a six-member act, first since Heeseung's departure

Heeseung left the group permanently in March, with label BELIFT LAB stating he "has a clear artistic direction he wants to pursue"; he has since gone by the solo name EVAN. The announcement of an August return — ENHYPEN's first promotional cycle as Jungwon, Jay, Jake, Sunghoon, Sunoo, and Ni-ki — made it one of the most closely watched comebacks of the year's second half.

Desk 02 · Review

4.1 / 5EDITOR'S STAMP
"A Brazilian-funk fever dream that treats danger and romance as the same rush — brief, brass-driven, and built almost entirely around a chorus of body rolls and footwork."

THE SIN: BLISS (2026): ten tracks, and the first ENHYPEN has released as a six-member group. Title track "Bloody Paradise" leans into brass synths and bass-heavy production, pairing deeper vocals against lighter harmonies for contrast. The lyrics frame lovers as prison escapees who find paradise in each other's company — dangerous, catchy, and over quickly.

The choreography is the clear highlight, with the chorus's footwork and hip movement doing most of the song's emotional work. Reviewers have noted a wish for the verses to spotlight individual members more, especially on a comeback this closely watched.
